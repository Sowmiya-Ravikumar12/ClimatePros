Equipment Findability Guide

How to use this. Every equipment item with real evidence follows the same four steps below — and every step's evidence comes only from the client's own Uploads (drawings, schedules, specs), never from Climate Pros' internal estimating workbook.

Cross-cutting findings — read this first

Nine patterns from checking dozens of jobs, plus Climate Pros' own bid-review records — these matter more than any single lookup below.

01 · Risk "Referenced but missing" sheets — confirmed on 7 jobs, 5 chains

The sheet index lists an Equipment Schedule, Responsibility Matrix, or detail sheet that isn't actually included in what the client sent — HEB 57's R-2.4, HEB 383's R-5.2, HEB Corp #271's R-2.0/R-2.2, Whole Foods 10593's G700, Kroger DAL653's R6.1, Safeway 2944's own general notes referencing an undelivered R9.1, and Sprouts Anna's piping plan citing a rack/condenser sheet "R6.0" that isn't anywhere in that job's set either. Always check the sheet index against what's physically in the upload before concluding a schedule doesn't exist — it may just be missing, and worth asking the client for.

02 · Confirmed pattern Sheet numbers vary by engineering firm; the document types underneath don't

Nearly every job with real drawings has some version of: an Equipment Schedule, a Circuit/Case Schedule, a Refrigerant Disclosure/Charge Schedule, a Responsibility Matrix, a Line-Size Schedule, a Details sheet, a Roof Plan, and a Controls/EMS sheet. Confirmed independently by Climate Pros' own "AI Client Bid Estimator Requirements" document, which lists this same document taxonomy as what the estimating tool needs to ingest from client Uploads.

03 · Terminology Same equipment, different names by refrigerant type and chain

TXV / "Expansion Valve" / EEV are the same part. Air-Cooled Condenser becomes a "Gas Cooler" on CO2 jobs. "Water Reclaim Tank" is always written as "Heat Reclaim Tank" in the actual specs (and sometimes a specific product name like "Doucette" instead). A Charging Station shows up as "Charge Savers" on one chain's drawings and as a bare panel-circuit tag ("CR-3") on another's.

04 · No schedule exists Some items are never on a drawing — the only evidence is a sentence

TXV Service, Comp Rack Service/Retrofit Ops, and Pipe Insulation Service typically have no dedicated schedule table anywhere in the uploads. The only evidence is a line in a Scope-of-Work narrative, a demolition-plan note, or a Responsibility Matrix describing the service operation (e.g. "if any rack systems are opened, replace filter cores"). If you're hunting for a schedule row for these three, stop looking — there usually isn't one; the count comes from that sentence, not a table.

05 · Watch for Some drawing sheets are rasterized images, not searchable text

Confirmed on HEB CORP #0833 and on Whole Foods 10593's own schedule sheet. A text search across the PDF comes back empty even though the schedule is right there — open the actual page and look before concluding it's missing.

06 · Not observed Four items never showed up on a single drawing, in any job checked

Porta Pack, New Tower Install (water side), Tower Circuits, and R&R Water-Cooled Condenser as its own scope. They're still real equipment categories — see items 5, 7, 8, and 9 below for what Climate Pros' own bid-review records say about them instead.

07 · Cost impact The real money is lost in count mismatches, not missing items

Climate Pros' own PMO bid-review records (June 2026) show the costliest recurring mistake isn't failing to find an equipment type — it's the count on the bid not matching the count on the schedule: Publix 2118's refrigeration schedule showed 85 cases, the bid carried 24; Walmart 3478's drawings showed 36 cases and 6 evaporators, the bid had 40 cases and zero evaporators; Walmart 2101's review found half the piping and insulation missing, a $393,092 correction. Whenever you find an item using this guide, count it against the schedule a second time before moving on — finding it once is not the same as counting it right.

08 · Watch for Filenames aren't always trustworthy — verify the title block, not the filename

On HEB 385 (a vendor-exported "CPQ" drawing set), the PDF named "...R-3.0 REFRIG CONTROLS NTS & SYMBS.pdf" actually contains the R-2.1 Refrigeration Schedule, and two files named for different demo sheets turned out to be byte-identical copies of an unrelated architectural wall-section detail. This looks like a one-off vendor export bug, not a chain-wide pattern — but it means a keyword search across filenames alone can send you to the wrong page. Open the sheet and read its own title block before trusting what the filename says it is.

09 · No correlation Job size doesn't predict document richness

HEB 566 — a single seafood-case swap, the smallest job checked — still arrived with the full formal HEB drawing set (cover sheet, demo plan, demo schedule, partial plan, Refrigeration Schedule, controls notes, four detail sheets, EMS sheets). Don't assume a small-sounding job means a thin Uploads folder; check the sheet index regardless of how minor the scope sounds.

11/4-10 HP Condensing Unit STRONG

1Where it sits

Sheet R-2.0, the lower of two stacked tables on the page — the upper table is the per-rack schedule (see Item 3); this lower one is headed by the cell "APPLIANCE NUMBER" and lists standalone condensing units one per row.

2On the sheet

3The exact text, as printed

Refrigeration Equipment Schedule — Appliance Number table (row example) Appliance Number SISF08 Description Seafood Ice Flaker (2K) Refrigeration Load 17,050 BTU/hr SST -7°F Refrigerant R449A Manufacturer Howe Compressor Model ZF15K4ETF5251 Condensing Unit Model HA2001-A-R449A-1-HG Dimension 30.5"x43.875"x29.25" Weight 323 LBS Liquid Line 1/2" Suction Line 1 1/8"

Eight rows total on this sheet (Appliance Numbers SIDA01 through SISF08). Every character above is copied straight off the printed table — nothing guessed.

4How you count it

Count one row = one unit. This sheet lists 8 rows, each with its own Condensing Unit Model number, so this job has 8 standalone condensing units in this class. The Appliance Number prefix ("SI") plus the Description column tells you which space each one serves (Dairy Cooler, Frozen Food Freezer, Meat Cooler, Seafood Ice Flaker) — that's what makes this a genuine condensing unit schedule and not just a box/space list, even though the description text alone can look like the latter at a glance.

210-35 HP Condensing Unit THIN

1Where it sits

The same Equipment Schedule table type as Item 1 — not a separate sheet, just a bigger HP value in the same compressor table. On a rack-based job it's a "Compressor Schedule" table with its own explicit HP column, one row per compressor.

2On the sheet

3The exact text, as printed

Group "A" Rack - Low Temp - R-407A — Compressor table (row example) Unit Designation LTA1 Compressor Mfr COPELAND Compressor Model 4DJXF76KL-TSK-C55 Modulation UNLOADER Capacity 66.5 MBH THR 97.6 MBH KW 4.40 Comp HP 12.5 EER 15.11 RLA (460/3/60) 28.3 A -- SYSTEM 'A' TOTALS: 42.00 HP across 4 compressors --

Individual compressors here ran 7.0-12.5 HP each; the rulebook's "10-35 HP" bucket is really a size range, not a printed label — you place the row into it based on this HP column, or the equivalent capacity/model number when no HP column exists.

4How you count it

Count whole units, not compressors. A single condensing unit whose one (or combined) compressor lands in the 10-35 HP band is 1 unit — a 4-compressor rack (like this one) isn't 4 units in this bucket, it's 1 rack, priced under Item 3 instead. Only count here when the schedule shows a genuinely standalone unit (its own Appliance Number/Mark, its own condensing-unit model) whose HP falls in this range.

3Comp Rack (HFC parallel) STRONG

1Where it sits

Sheet R-2.0, the upper table — columns are the rack names themselves (Rack A / Rack B / Rack C), with manufacturer, model, refrigerant, and electrical data running down the rows for each. A "Compressor Schedule" variant lists the same rack as a row-per-compressor table instead.

2On the sheet

Naming variant — "Compressor Schedule" (row-per-compressor)

3The exact text, as printed

Compressor Schedule — System LTA DX (row example) System LTA DX Compressor # 2 Model 4DJNF76KLTSKC23 NRE 59 MBH % of Required Load 33.1% THR 89.7 MBH Variable Capacity? NO HP 12.5 RPM 1750 RLA 32.1 LRA 187 -- LTA DX TOTAL: 44.5 HP across 4 compressors --

Identifier = the rack/system name itself (LTA DX), not a serial number — every compressor under it shares that same tag.

4How you count it

Count distinct rack names/columns, not compressors. The HEB CORP #0833 sheet names 3 racks (A/B/C) — that's 3 racks, regardless of how many compressors sit inside each one. On the Compressor Schedule variant, count the number of different System names (LTA DX, MTAS DX, MTB DX, etc.) the same way; the compressor rows underneath each one are detail, not a separate count. Cross-check the rack's own refrigerant charge against a Refrigerant Disclosure/Charge Schedule where one exists, rather than assuming a single default value applies to every rack.

4CO2 Cascade Comp Rack STRONG

1Where it sits

The same rack table as Item 3 — look for the phrase "CO2 Cascade Split" in the System Application row of one rack's column. There's no separate sheet for it; it's a CO2-flavored row inside the ordinary rack schedule.

2On the sheet

3The exact text, as printed

Rack C column, same Refrigeration Equipment Schedule System Application CO2 CASCADE SPLIT Rack Model CO2 CASCADE 300/600 SPLIT TEMP Refrigerant R513A&R744

4How you count it

It's one more rack in the same count as Item 3 — don't count it twice or file it separately. Rack C above is one of the 3 racks total on this sheet. What's different about a CO2 cascade rack is that its Refrigerant row lists two refrigerants (a high-side HFC blend and R-744/CO2) instead of one — both need their own charge amount, which comes from the job's Refrigerant Disclosure/Charge Schedule, not this Equipment Schedule row. If a rack's Refrigerant field lists two refrigerants joined by "&", that's your signal you're looking at a cascade system, not a plain single-refrigerant rack.

5Porta Pack (packaged rack) NOT OBSERVED ON A DRAWING

Never found on a drawing in any of the 24 folders checked. No PDF in this dataset contains the term "Porta Pack" or an equivalent packaged-rack schedule row. Kroger's spec has the closest relative — a "Prefabricated Mechanical Center" (Section 11 41 43, Article 3.3.B) — but that's Kroger's own related-but-different term, not a confirmed sighting.
But it's real enough to have cost real money. Climate Pros' own PMO bid-review records (Publix 2118) list Portapack as one of the items missed from an empty equipment tab, alongside 3 water-cooled condensers and filter/drier changes — so this item does show up on real jobs, this dataset's drawing sets simply didn't happen to include one. If a job's Equipment Schedule lists any kind of fully-packaged, self-contained rack unit (its own Mark/Appliance Number, its own model, described as shipping complete rather than field-assembled from a rack + separate condenser), that's the profile to watch for.

6Air-Cooled Condenser (12-fan class) STRONG

1Where it sits

Its own "Condenser Schedule" block, either inside the combined Equipment Schedule sheet or as its own dedicated "Equipment Schedule" table on a fixture sheet (coded "F1" on the CapX condenser-replacement jobs).

2On the sheet

Naming variant — CO2 systems call it a "Gas Cooler"

3The exact text, as printed

Condenser Equipment Schedule (row example) Mark MTB DX Manufacturer Hill Phoenix Model BNH-D08-A068 Refrigerant R-404A Fan Quantity 8 Gas Cooler Schedule (CO2 variant, row example) Rack RACK DTA Manufacturer BAC Model TSDC2-CO2-202-12.4 System THR 571.7 MBH Spare Capacity 192.3 MBH (33.64%)

The fan count is often baked into the model number itself rather than spelled out as its own "12-fan" label — that literal phrase was never found verbatim anywhere in this dataset.

4How you count it

Count real standalone condensers only — check the rack's own "Condenser Mounting" field first. If the rack schedule (Item 3) says "Condenser Mounting: Integrated" for a given rack, its condenser is already built into that rack — don't count it again here. Only count a condenser on this item when it has its own Mark and model, separate from any rack, the way the El Campo condensers above do (one row = one condenser).

7New Tower Install (water side) NOT OBSERVED

Never found — 0 for 24 folders, including a dedicated re-check. Zero occurrences of "cooling tower" tied to actual project scope anywhere, including a Walmart "Mechanical Schedules" (M3) sheet specifically checked because HVAC schedules sometimes carry tower content — it had only an Air Device Schedule and a small heat-pump split-system schedule, no tower. A generic HVAC abbreviations legend on one Sprouts job defines "CT = Cooling Tower" among dozens of stock abbreviations, but it isn't tied to any schedule, tag, or installed unit on that job. Every CO2/SCO2 job in this dataset uses air-cooled gas coolers instead.

If this comes up on a real job: expect it on a Mechanical Schedule (Division 23, not the refrigeration-specific sheets), plus a Roof/Site Plan for placement — a real tower would show its own Mark, manufacturer, and tonnage/GPM rating there the same way a condenser schedule shows a condenser.

8Tower Circuits (refrigerant charge only) NOT OBSERVED

Never found. Same reason as Item 7 — no cooling tower jobs exist in this dataset, so there's no tower-circuit charge-only line to point to.

9R&R Water-Cooled Condenser NOT OBSERVED ON A DRAWING

Never found on a drawing. The 3 Walmart "Condenser Replacement" CapX jobs in this dataset are all confirmed air-cooled swaps. Every condenser reference checked elsewhere is explicitly air-cooled too (Safeway 2944's schedule literally says "Condenser Type: Air" on every circuit). The only water-cooled hits anywhere were a generic commissioning-checklist line and a "self-contained water-cooled cases" product mention — neither an R&R scope item.
But — same as Porta Pack — it's real enough to show up in a bid-review loss. The same Publix 2118 review that missed Portapack also missed 3 water-cooled condensers. If a demo plan flags an existing condenser for removal and the equipment schedule doesn't explicitly say "air-cooled," don't assume — check for a water-side connection (isolation/check valves at a water loop, a condenser-water piping plan) before pricing it as an air-cooled swap.
Close relative worth knowing about — the adiabatic/hybrid air-cooled condenser. Sprouts Cibolo's plumbing details (P401/P402, "Refrigeration Condenser Make-Up Water") show a condenser type this dataset hadn't seen before: an air-cooled condenser with a water-spray pre-cooler pad, EMS-controlled to fill at 40°F ambient and drain at 45°F. It's water-assisted, not water-cooled or tower-fed — don't file it under this item, but do expect a small domestic-water connection and an EMS solenoid valve on the plumbing plan if you see one, since it's easy to mistake for either a true water-cooled condenser or a plain air-cooled one.

10-11New Stub — Gas / Off-time-Electric THIN

1Where it sits

A row inside the ordinary Circuit Schedule — spare/future circuits get their own row alongside the active ones, plus a matching floor-plan keynote.

2On the sheet

3The exact text, as printed

Refrigeration Schedule System MTB — circuit row example CKT # B04 DX Description Spare Stub Comment New Spare Stub for Future Use Defrost Type OT CKT # B09 DX Defrost Type EL

Whether it's a gas (hot-gas defrost) stub or an off-time/electric stub is read off that same row's Defrost Type column — EL for electric, OT for off-time.

4How you count it

Count "Spare Stub" rows, split by their Defrost Type column. Each spare-stub row with Defrost Type = OT is one off-time/electric stub; each with a hot-gas defrost designation is a gas stub. Count them separately — they price differently (a hot-gas stub needs a brazed gas-line tie-in, an off-time/electric stub doesn't).

12TXV Service STRONG

1Where it sits

A column inside the Circuit Schedule on new-construction jobs, one value per circuit. On a pure service/retrofit job, there's usually no schedule at all — the count comes straight from a Scope-of-Work sentence instead (see cross-cutting finding #4).

2On the sheet

Naming variant — "Expansion Valve" / EEV

3The exact text, as printed

Refrigeration Schedule System LTA — circuit row example CKT # A04 DX Evaporator Coil TXV / Distributor Nozzle EGVE-3-Z / #2 Symbol Legend (variant sheet) Symbol [schematic valve symbol] Label ELECTRONIC EXPANSION VALVE

Watch the terminology — 2 of 3 Walmart CapX folders never use the literal word "TXV," calling it "Expansion Valve" or "Holdback Valve" instead; Whole Foods calls it an EEV and explicitly notes "TXV NOT ACCEPTABLE" on one schedule.

4How you count it

On new construction, count circuit rows in the TXV/Expansion Valve column — one per circuit. On a service job, there's no schedule row to count at all; the quantity is whatever the Scope of Work states (e.g. "replace TXV on 5 circuits"), and whether it's a full body change (brazed, system opened) versus a cartridge/power-head swap (mechanical only, no braze) has to be read directly out of that same SOW language, not assumed.

13Water Reclaim Tank THIN

1Where it sits

A header field inside the main Refrigeration System Schedule — every schedule checked has a place for it ("H/R Media," "H/R Use," "H/R Water GPM," "H/R Glycol Type/%"), but on every job checked so far those fields print as blank dashes. The spec's own name for it is "Heat Reclaim Tank," not "Water Reclaim Tank."

3The exact text, as printed

Section 20 69 93 — Sequence of Operations for Refrigeration Controls (HEB CORP #0833) "Heat Reclaim Tank(s) provides supplemental heating to the water heater by preheating refrigerant. Heat reclaim mode activates 3-way valve to divert high temperature discharge vapor refrigerant away from condenser into the heat reclaim tank(s)." Refrigeration System Schedule header fields (confirmed blank on Walmart 0383-277) H/R Media: -- H/R Use: -- H/R Water GPM: -- H/R Glycol Type/%: --

Whole Foods 10593 names an actual product for it (Specifications §223530, "Heat Reclaim Tanks," Lochinvar) and notes the rack OEM supplies the heat exchanger/pump/3-way valve factory-installed at the rack. HEB 385 names a different specific product for the same underlying function — a "Doucette" heat exchanger/desuperheater mounted on the rack itself, with a general note that "Kysor Warren will supply/install the Doucette heat exchanger... plumbing contractor responsible for water connections." Same equipment category, a third distinct product name — add "Doucette" to what you search for.

4How you count it

Count 1 only if the schedule's H/R fields are actually filled in — leave it at zero if they print as dashes. A blank H/R row means this job's rack doesn't reclaim heat at all; don't infer a tank exists just because the spec generically describes what one would do. When it is filled in, the tank count and its sizing come from that same schedule row (or the plumbing water-heater schedule's own reclaim-tank line, if a separate one exists).

14Protocol / Proto-Aire THIN

1Where it sits

Chain-specific: strongly present on Kroger, essentially absent everywhere else. It shows up on the floor plan as a bare keynote, not a schedule row with a count column — plus a dedicated spec section on Kroger jobs.

3The exact text, as printed

Section 11 41 63 — Fixture and Equipment Electrical Installation, "3.5 Protocol Refrigeration Equipment" (Kroger DAL653) "Protocol units are a remote compressor unit that is placed in various locations throughout the store as indicated on the Fixture and Refrigeration Drawings. Each protocol unit is furnished with a branch circuit and compressor panel located in the front door of the unit... These remote compressors work in conjunction with a fluid cooler, pump station and/or air cooled condenser." Floor plan keynote (same job) "PROTOCOL AND ASSOC. EQUIPMENT. RE: PLUMBING & ELECTRICAL DRAWINGS FOR ADDITIONAL INFORMATION."

Walmart 0395 mentions "Protocol" only once, as a defined term inside a "Compressor House" glossary note, with no actual unit scheduled. No hits at all on the HEB, Whole Foods, Safeway, or Carrs folders checked.

EQUIPMENT" callout on the fixture/refrigeration drawings is one unit; cross-reference against the electrical drawings' branch-circuit count for that unit type to confirm the number.

15EVAC System (mains & risers) STRONG

1Where it sits

A procedural section in the specification document — described as a process, not a schedule table with a quantity column. Actual PVC main/copper riser footage comes from the piping/floor plan instead.

2On the sheet

3The exact text, as printed

Section 20 63 96 — Refrigeration System Evacuation and Charging, 3.2/3.3 "3. Connect the vacuum pump to three points on the compressor rack. Connect to two points on condensing units." ... "A. Evacuate system to 1,000 microns (±50 microns) and close the vacuum header valve."

Not tagged/counted in the schedule sense — identified by connection points on the rack/condensing unit described in the procedure text.

4How you count it

There's no count in the spec itself — it's a per-rack/per-unit procedure, not a line item. What you can count: the PVC main and copper/PVC riser footage, read off the piping/line-size schedule under the same rack name as any other refrigerant piping (see Item 20) — that's where the actual linear footage for evacuation mains and risers comes from, not this spec section.

16Comp Rack - Service/Retrofit Ops STRONG

1Where it sits

Triggered by a note on the demolition plan, Responsibility Matrix, or BAS sheet describing a retrofit — there is no dedicated schedule table for this item anywhere in the uploads checked.

2On the sheet

3The exact text, as printed

General notes box, Refrigeration BAS One-Line Diagram "COORDINATE WITH LEGACY BAS VENDOR (NOVAR) FOR THE REPROGRAMMING OF THEIR RACK CONTROLLER WITH THE REMOVAL OF REFRIGERATION CIRCUITS FROM THEIR CONTROL. VERIFY DEFROST SCHEDULES ARE COORDINATED BETWEEN THE EXISTING RACK CONTROLLER AND THE NEW CASE AND WALK-IN CONTROLS."

Not tagged with its own line item on the drawing — it's a plain-English coordination instruction.

4How you count it

Count service operations, not parts — read the operation count straight from the note or demo-plan scope, not from a schedule. A note like this one describes a single reprogramming operation for the rack being retrofitted; if the same job's demo plan also calls for changing rack filters/driers "if any rack systems are opened," that's a separate, distinct operation to count once per rack actually opened — the exact wording on the demo plan or Responsibility Matrix is the only source for how many times each operation happens.

17Pipe Insulation Service THIN

1Where it sits

A generic insulation-material table in the spec, organized by pipe size and service temperature — there is no dedicated "replace insulation" schedule or count column anywhere in the uploads checked; the actual linear footage has to come from the piping plan plus the Scope of Work.

2On the sheet

3The exact text, as printed

Schedule I — Piping Insulation, Part 2.4-B Service R404A/R407A/R448A/R422D - Suction Piping Size 1-5/8 inches and larger Piping Material Type L Hard Drawn Copper, Sealed ACR Tubing Insulation 1-1/2 inch

By pipe size and refrigerant band, not by a count. The R744/CO2 rows of this same schedule continue onto the next spec page.

4How you count it

Read linear feet off the piping plan for whichever existing runs the Scope of Work says get new insulation, then look up the required thickness for that pipe size/service band on this table. The spec table tells you what insulation is required per size; it never states a footage total by itself — that comes from measuring the actual runs called out for re-insulation on the plan.

18Display Cases (lineups) STRONG

1Where it sits

The single most reliably findable item in this whole guide — case tags on the floor plan plus a matching row in the Circuit/Case Schedule, on every job with real drawings.

2On the sheet

3The exact text, as printed

Refrigerated Circuit Summary / Case-Coil Information (row example) Sys. No. RACB01 Description Curbside Cooler Case/Coil Manufacturer Krack Model # GHD56D-340NDBA

Tagged by department-prefixed circuit code, with a Description column naming the real fixture and a Case/Coil Manufacturer + Model column alongside it.

4How you count it

Count case tags on the floor plan, then group consecutive same-run circuits into lineups, cross-checked against the schedule's own row count. The schedule's Length/Doors and fixture-length columns tell you how each individual case is sized; the floor plan tells you which ones sit end-to-end as one physical lineup. Count both and make sure they agree — this is exactly the kind of count that gets lost between drawing and bid per finding #7 above.

19Walk-in Coolers/Freezers + Evap Coils STRONG

1Where it sits

Columns inside the same main schedule used for cases — look for "Walk-In Box Size" and "WI Evaps" columns — or, on jobs with no formal schedule, bare room-dimension callouts on the floor plan.

2On the sheet

3The exact text, as printed

Refrigeration System Schedule, System DTA DX (row example) CKT # AS01a DX Walk-In Box Size 1/2 Dairy Cooler W.I. w/ drs. Case/Coil Model KR34A-173 Manufacturer KRACK LSHX N

By the same circuit tag as any other case row; when no formal schedule exists, the fallback is a bare room label directly on the floor plan (e.g. "DAIRY VAULT / 47'-0" X 15'-7" X 10'-0" HT.").

4How you count it

Count coils by circuit row, count boxes by room label — they aren't the same number. A single walk-in box can carry sub-circuits (this row's "1/2 Dairy Cooler" notation means the box is split across two circuits), so count evaporator coils from the schedule's coil/model column, and count physical walk-in boxes separately from the floor plan's room labels or dimensioned box outlines.

20Refrigerant Piping (system) STRONG

1Where it sits

A dedicated Piping/Line-Size Schedule — essentially universal wherever real drawings exist, and the most consistently tagged item in this dataset.

2On the sheet

3The exact text, as printed

Rack RA Line Size Schedule (MT), Loop 1 (row example) Trunkline 1A Liquid 1-1/8" Suction 2-5/8" Suction Riser 1-5/8"-1-3/8" Actual Line Length 106' Equiv. Line Length 138'

4How you count it

Count every branch/trunk row on the schedule and read its size and length straight off that row. Each row is one pipe segment (a trunkline or a numbered branch like RA02/RA02A); the schedule already separates liquid, suction, and suction-riser sizes and gives both an actual and an equivalent (fitting-adjusted) length, so no measurement or waste-factor math is needed to identify what's there — that math happens downstream, this schedule is the source of truth for what to measure against.

21Glycol / Secondary Loop STRONG

Upgraded from "not observed" once a real CO2-Glycol Upgrade job (Walmart 0383-277, Silsbee TX) came through. That job adds a brand-new secondary glycol loop ("MTB GLY") alongside a transcritical CO2 conversion, with a genuinely rich schedule set: a dedicated pump-skid schedule, a refrigerant-charge schedule showing the actual loop volume, glycol-specific piping/detail sheets, and glycol circuit tags all the way through the BAS/electrical drawings.

1Where it sits

A "Secondary Pump Skid Schedule" inside the same Equipment Schedule sheet set, plus its own glycol-specific rows on the Refrigerant Charge Schedule, Piping Schedule, and Responsibility Matrix (a "GLY SKID" column sits alongside "Existing Racks," "TCO2 Pack/Rack," and "SCO2 Skid").

2On the sheet

3The exact text, as printed

Refrigeration Secondary Pump Skid Schedule (row example) Mark MTB GLY Manufacturer RAECorp Model WMT-SEC-GLY-32-160 Location Ground Primary Refrigerant R404a Secondary Refrigerant 35% Glycol System Load 169.3 MBH Min/Max Flow Rate 32 / 160 GPM Voltage 460V 3Ph 60Hz Refrigerant Charge Schedule (same job) Mark MTB GLY Unit of Measure GAL New Refrigerant Type 35% GLY Estimated New Refrigerant Type Added 203.2 gal Total Estimated New Charge 203.2 gal

The loop volume (203.2 gallons of 35% propylene glycol) is disclosed directly on the Refrigerant Charge Schedule — the same sheet type used for refrigerant lbs on an HFC/CO2 job, just in gallons for a glycol loop.

4How you count it

Count 1 skid per Mark on the Pump Skid Schedule, and read the fluid volume straight off the Refrigerant Charge Schedule — in gallons, not lbs. Detail sheets for this job also carry glycol-specific construction details ("Islander Case Piping (Glycol)," "Temporary U-Bend Bypass (Glycol)," "End-of-Loop Bypass Valve (Glycol)") — each one is a real, distinct piece of scope worth checking off against the Responsibility Matrix's "GLY SKID" column rather than assuming it's covered by whatever a plain CO2 or HFC job's detail sheets already show.

22EMS / Controls System STRONG

1Where it sits

A dedicated Controls/BAS sheet (coded "BASR" or "R-3.x"), usually a symbol legend plus one or more setpoint/schedule tables.

2On the sheet

Naming variant — Symbol Legend format

3The exact text, as printed

EMS Symbol Legend (excerpt) RA Return Air Temperature Sensor DA Discharge Air Temperature Sensor LT Liquid Temperature Sensor ST Suction Temperature Sensor PT Pressure Transducer EEV Electronic Expansion Valve LLSV Liquid Line Solenoid Valve

By controller brand (Novar, KE2, Sporlan, CPC all appear across different jobs) and point/sensor symbol code. A "Leak Detection Equipment Schedule" often sits right next to the main controls table and should be counted alongside it.

4How you count it

The legend defines what each symbol means — it doesn't itself give a count. Tally how many times each symbol actually appears on the Controls floor plan (BASR1/BASR2-style sheets) to get real device counts, then cross-reference the Responsibility Matrix to confirm who furnishes/programs each device type before pricing anything — that furnish/install split is stated on the matrix, not the legend.
