Model Evaluation

Which OpenAI model should read refrigeration & construction drawings and propose equipment quantities — evaluated across accuracy, cost, and the failure modes that matter when the output feeds a customer quotation.

1. What the model actually has to do

The deliverable is a quotation, but the model never sees a price. It sees a drawing and has to answer one question correctly, repeatedly, at scale: what equipment is here, and how many? That number flows unchanged into a deterministic pricing calculator downstream — a miscount isn't a stylistic flaw, it's a wrong number on a bid.

Concretely, the model has to:

  • Find and count equipment symbols on dense, often large-format, sometimes poorly scanned sheets
  • Read tabular equipment schedules and reconcile them against what the floor plan shows
  • Compute a real-world length (e.g. run of pipe) from a drawing's stated scale
  • Return a confidence signal honest enough to decide whether a human needs to look at it
  • Cross-reference the same physical item appearing on more than one sheet, without double-counting it

Given that, the metric that should decide this evaluation is detection and counting accuracy — not general writing quality, and not benchmark scores unrelated to finding and counting objects in an image.

4. Recommendation

A two-tier pipeline: GPT-5.6 Terra for triage, GPT-5.6 Sol for extraction.

No single model is both cheap and maximally accurate. Splitting the work by difficulty gets Sol's accuracy where an error would reach a customer's price, without paying Sol's cost and latency on every page of a large drawing package.

  • Stage 1 — Terra: runs on every uploaded page. Classifies sheet type, checks legibility, sorts what the package contains. Cheap, fast, doesn't need frontier accuracy.
  • Stage 2 — Sol: runs on the pages that matter — the actual equipment identification, counting, and scale-based measurement. Escalated to automatically wherever Terra's confidence is low.

Why two models, not one: the tasks in this workflow are not equally hard. Classifying what kind of sheet a page is is a simpler problem than finding and correctly counting every piece of equipment on it. Paying the most expensive model's cost and latency for the easy step wastes both, and a confidence-gated escalation gets Sol's accuracy exactly where it is needed without paying for it everywhere else:

Every page → Terra classifies & triages → confident pages proceed as-is; uncertain pages escalate to Sol for extraction & counting → validated equipment counts.

5. Why not the alternatives

Each was evaluated on its own merits, not dismissed by default. Here is the specific reason each falls short of Sol for the extraction step.

Terra & Luna

Not disqualified — reassigned. Both trail Sol on detection (44.7 and 43.3 vs. 46.2) and neither has a published counting-accuracy figure at all, against Sol’s measured 73.0%. On the one step where an error becomes a wrong price, that gap is worth Sol’s extra cost. Their real value is the cheaper triage pass, not the extraction itself.

GPT-5.5

13.8 mAP vs. Sol’s 46.2 — not a marginal gap, a different tier of capability at the exact skill this task needs most. Its OCR is fine, even slightly ahead of Sol’s, but OCR was never the bottleneck; finding and counting equipment is.

GPT-5.4 family

No detection or counting benchmark has been published for this generation at all. Given the model immediately preceding it (5.5) scored 13.8 mAP, there is no basis to assume 5.4 did meaningfully better. Cheap and unproven is not a trade worth making on the step that determines pricing accuracy.

GPT-5.2, 5.1, GPT-5-family, o3, o3-pro

All pre-date the release cycle where detection and counting were specifically targeted and fixed — none has published evidence of clearing that bar. o3 additionally burns 5,000–20,000 internal reasoning tokens per answer billed at output rates, so its real cost can run 10–15× the headline price with no accuracy evidence to justify it.

GPT-4.1

The closest real trade-off — cheap, vision-capable, huge context window. But it has no reasoning-effort control at all (“the smartest non-reasoning model”). Reading a scale bar to compute a real-world length, or reconciling a schedule against a floor plan, are multi-step reasoning problems. A model that cannot be told to think harder on a hard sheet has no way to signal it is struggling — it answers confidently either way.

GPT-4o & mini

A solid, well-established baseline, but a much smaller context window (128K vs. 1.05M) makes cross-referencing a large multi-sheet package harder, and it predates every detection improvement above. No reason to prefer it over Sol on accuracy, or over Terra/Luna on cost.

gpt-oss-120b & 20b

Not a close call. Confirmed text-only — no image input whatsoever. Cannot see the drawing, regardless of reasoning quality.

6. Known limitations of Sol

Recommending Sol is not the same as trusting it blind. These are the specific, evidenced weaknesses to engineer around:

  1. Unreliable on large images — degrades above roughly 2,000×2,000 pixels, placing bounding boxes in the wrong parts of the image at lower reasoning effort. Real construction scans are frequently this size or larger — sheets must be resized or tiled before submission.
  2. Coordinate-format sensitivity — omitting an explicit instruction to return absolute XYXY pixel coordinates costs roughly 15 mAP points.
  3. Weak on small, low-contrast text — text-extraction accuracy dropped versus the prior generation (87.6%→82.5%), especially on small, low-contrast, or oddly oriented text — exactly the profile of dimension callouts and pipe/circuit tags on real drawings.
  4. Most expensive, slowest tier — ~2.5¢ and ~10 seconds per image at $5/$30 per million tokens. Running every page of a hundred-plus-page package through Sol is both costly and slow.
  5. Counting still imperfect on hard cases — 73.0% is the best published score, but that still means roughly one in four counts needs correction, particularly on visually similar items differing only in subtle state.
  6. No domain-specific benchmark exists — every figure above comes from general vision benchmarks, none tests construction or engineering drawings specifically.

7. Required before production use

Not a drop-in decision — three things need to happen first: resize/tile drawings before submission (Sol degrades on large images), standardize prompts to request exact pixel coordinates, and validate against real Climate Pros drawing samples before committing in production.
